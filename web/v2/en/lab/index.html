Mint address

It is the address of the token’s mint account. It identifies that mint on the network named in the card. The name and the symbol are separate text.

Example. On the 2026-10-16 cards, EPjFWdd5AufqSSqeM2qN1xzybapC8G4wEGGkZwyTDt1v (USDC) and 5vJmAfBjsS6wEHcgQGNm69P3fbyPmUU1dVfE5j1rraVZ are different addresses even though both are named “USD Coin”.

It does not let you conclude: It does not name a person, and it does not say that any action with the token is advisable. Two equal names are not the same address.

Curated registry

A local list Verify compares with the mint, name, symbol, image, and some links. In the examples it includes USDC. ORVUPI is not on it. A mint that is not on the list is not necessarily fake.

Example. USDT, on the 2026-10-16 card, is not in the registry. The card itself says that does not mean it is false or a copy.

It does not let you conclude: Being in the registry is not an audit or a guarantee. Being absent is not an accusation either.

Mint authority

Permission to increase the supply of that mint. If the card says revoked and the field was read on-chain, the field that was read is empty (no authority set). If it says active, the permission is still assigned to an address.

Example. In the 2026-10-16 batch, the three “USD Coin” copies have it revoked. USDC and USDT have it active.

It does not let you conclude: Revoked does not identify the mint, does not show that the project is legitimate, and says nothing about demand or liquidity. An unavailable field is not filled in as revoked.

Freeze authority

Permission to freeze accounts of that token. “Revoked” means that specific permission was removed at the time of reading. Active means it is still assigned.

Example. In the same batch, the registry mint has it revoked and USDC has it active, assigned to an address the card shows.

It does not let you conclude: It is not a guarantee or proof of identity. The absence of this permission does not rule out other restrictions on the token.

Mutable metadata

If there is an update authority or is_mutable is true, the name, symbol, or image can change. “Not mutable in the sources read” describes those sources on that date.

Example. On 2026-10-16, the 5vJmAfBjsS6wEHcgQGNm69P3fbyPmUU1dVfE5j1rraVZ copy is recorded as not mutable in the sources read. USDC has is_mutable true in Metaplex.

It does not let you conclude: That they cannot be changed in that reading does not show legitimacy. That they can be changed is not, by itself, impersonation.

Unknown and unavailable

Unavailable means that call did not leave a usable fact. Unknown is what was not read. Neither one is a value read on-chain.

Example. On all five cards from 2026-10-16, getTokenLargestAccounts returned HTTP 429. The token account sample stays unavailable. It is not zero concentration.

It does not let you conclude: It does not become zero, a revoked authority, or immutable metadata. A partial card also does not cancel the fields that were read on-chain.

Pump.fun curve

If the derived account belongs to the curve program, Verify reads its public fields. Classic progress is an inferred calculation, not a field of the account. If there is no curve, the curve amounts (Pump.fun calls these ‘reserves’) are not filled in with zero.

Example. None of the 2026-10-16 cards is a Pump.fun curve: on USDC, USDT, and the copies, progress stays not applicable. On a token that is on the curve, progress is inferred from the real amount left.

It does not let you conclude: Progress does not say what to do. Inferred 0.00 does not mean the account was missing. This mission does not simulate a swap.

Real curve amount

The real_token_reserves or real_quote_reserves field of the curve account, in base units. It is different from the virtual curve amount.

Example. On the classic curve, the documented initial real amount is 793100000000000. If a card reads exactly that figure, inferred progress is 0.00: nothing has been sold from the curve.

It does not let you conclude: It is not an audit of funds, and this page does not compute it as a withdrawable balance. If the field is absent, a zero is not invented.

Virtual curve amount

The virtual_token_reserves or virtual_quote_reserves field. The card keeps it apart from the real curve amount. On the classic curve, and with the default quote, the quote amount is in lamports.

Example. On a card for a token on the curve, the virtual and the real amounts of tokens appear as different numbers, each with its own status. The virtual one is part of the curve calculation; it cannot be withdrawn.

It does not let you conclude: The virtual curve amount is not the withdrawable balance. Adding it to the real curve amount does not create one available pool.

Possible impersonation

A Verify signal: the name, symbol, image, or a link matches the curated registry and the mint is a different one. The match does not attribute intent.

Example. The 2026-10-16 cards for 4XRbnGgay2XpqJ1F5jGPQ9pRh1JSX12RPCPJASMJU5JW, 5vJmAfBjsS6wEHcgQGNm69P3fbyPmUU1dVfE5j1rraVZ, and H5b7sHTqr7WJqL9X4io8VeTBfePSyv85wEBnKNaZKV9R carry that signal: they are named “USD Coin” and are not the USDC mint. USDC and USDT do not.

It does not let you conclude: It is not a verdict or proof of what someone meant to do. A similar name, by itself, also does not replace reading the address.

Account owner and person

An address stored on an account, such as the curve creator, is a public field. It does not identify a person or a legal owner.

Example. The registry mint card records the curve creator and says that pubkey does not identify a person.

It does not let you conclude: This mission cannot turn an address into a person's name. It does not ask for personal data.

‘Holder’ (industry term)

Example. An account that holds tokens.

Card

A card is the dated reading of an address. It does not update itself and it is not an audit.

Example. The cards in this mission are dated 2026-10-16.

It does not let you conclude: A card does not say what to do with the token or who a person is.

Census

A census would be the list of every account. These cards are not that: they read some accounts and do not fill the rest in with a zero.

Example. No 2026-10-16 card has the account sample: the service returned HTTP 429. Even if it had, it would be at most 20 accounts: it is not a census.

It does not let you conclude: Not being a census does not turn the missing fact into a zero.

Fee

A fee rule set by a connector (for example a DEX or an aggregator) on a swap. This mission does not calculate fees or swaps.

Example. The cards used here read public curve fields. They do not include a fee calculation, and this page does not add one.

It does not let you conclude: A fee does not identify the mint, does not turn the virtual curve amount into a withdrawable balance, and is not a promised result.

Stablecoin

A token meant to follow a currency. On these cards, USDC and USDT are the examples.

Example. On 2026-10-16, USDC has mint and freeze permissions active. The 5vJm… copy named “USD Coin” has them closed. The same permission is not read the same way on both.

It does not let you conclude: It does not say what to do with the token. An active permission on USDC is not an accusation, and a closed one on a copy is not a guarantee.

Technical account and pool

A technical account belongs to a program, such as the curve. A pool is the account where an exchange program holds tokens. Neither one is a person's account.

Example. On a token that is still on a launch curve, the account with the most tokens is usually the curve account: it belongs to the program, not a person. If the curve was completed, the large balance may be in a pool.

It does not let you conclude: A large balance does not turn that account into a person. Not being a pool does not say what to do with the token.

Failed query

The fact was asked for and the service left no usable answer. It is not a zero and it is not an absence.

Example. USDC, on 2026-10-16, asked for getTokenLargestAccounts and got HTTP 429. The sample stays unavailable.

It does not let you conclude: It is not filled in with zero, and it does not say that accounts do not exist.

Confirmed absent

The fact was asked for, the answer arrived, and the fact is not there. It is not a service failure.

Example. On USDC, on 2026-10-16, the metadata account was queried. The query was answered and the link (URI) is empty: that fact is not there.

It does not let you conclude: It is not an HTTP 429, and it is not a fact this reading forgot to ask for.

Not queried

This reading did not ask for that fact. It is not a failure and it is not an absence.

Example. The USDC card from 2026-10-16 has no URI, so it does not ask for the metadata JSON. The JSON name stays “not applicable”.

It does not let you conclude: It does not become zero, and it does not become the failed query on another card.

Guides

Identify the token

The name, the symbol, and the image are text and a file. The mint address is something else: it is the account of that token.

The Verify cards from 2026-10-16 include one mint in the curated registry, USDC's (EPjFWdd5AufqSSqeM2qN1xzybapC8G4wEGGkZwyTDt1v), and three others also named “USD Coin” whose address is different. The card calls that match possible impersonation. It does not attribute intent to anyone.

USDC is in the same batch and is not in the registry. The card says that does not mean it is false or a copy. Absence from the list is not an accusation. Presence on the list is not an audit.

For a new case, hypothetical or not, the order is the same: read the full address, compare it with the registry that applies, and do not replace it with the name, even if the name contains words such as official or community.

Cards have a UTC date and time. A later query creates another id. What could not be read stays unavailable and does not become a fact.

Read permissions

A permission is not a guarantee. Mint authority, if active, can increase the supply of that mint. If the card marks it revoked and the field was read on-chain, that specific permission is recorded as empty (none) in that reading.

Freeze authority is a different permission: freezing accounts of that token. Revoked removes it in that reading. Active leaves it assigned to the address the card shows.

In the 2026-10-16 batch, the three “USD Coin” copies have both authorities revoked. USDC has them active. The same permission on two addresses does not make them the same mint. A revoked permission also does not show that the project is legitimate, and it says nothing about demand or liquidity.

Mutable metadata is a separate piece. If there is an update authority or is_mutable is true, the name, the symbol, or the image can change. “Not mutable in the sources read” covers only those sources and that date. USDC has mutable metadata and that does not make it a copy of anything.

If a field is unavailable, it is not filled in as revoked or as immutable. Unknown is not the same as checked.

A hypothetical example, not read from the chain: a token with mint authority revoked can still have no liquidity and no demand. The permission predicts neither.

Understand the curve

On these cards, the Pump.fun curve is a derived account. If the owner is the curve program, Verify reads public fields: virtual and real curve amounts, the supply on that account, and whether complete is true or false.

The virtual curve amount is not the real curve amount. They are not added together as one withdrawable pool. The card shows them separately.

Classic curve progress is an inferred calculation: it starts from the documented initial real curve amount, 793100000000000, and truncates toward zero to two decimals. It is not a field stored on the account. If the real amount read matches that initial value, the subtraction is zero and progress comes out as inferred 0.00: the account was read. An inferred zero is not a missing fact. The 2026-10-16 cards (USDC, USDT, and three copies) are not Pump.fun curves: there, progress stays “not applicable”.

USDC has no curve at the derived address. The card records “not a curve” and progress stays not applicable. It does not fill the curve amounts with zero.

The token account sample on all five cards is unavailable because of HTTP 429. It is not zero concentration and it does not say who a person is. An account can be a custodian. The curve creator address does not identify a person either: it is a field of the account.

Market cap is not the curve’s liquidity. This mission does not calculate market cap, fees, or a swap. A progress figure is not an instruction.
