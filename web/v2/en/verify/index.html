Mint address

It is the address of the token’s mint account. It identifies that mint on the network named in the card. The name and the symbol are separate text.

Example. USDC on Solana is EPjFWdd5AufqSSqeM2qN1xzybapC8G4wEGGkZwyTDt1v. Another token called “USD Coin” at a different address is not USDC, even if the name is the same.

It does not let you conclude: It does not name a person, and it does not say that any action with the token is advisable. Two equal names are not the same address.

Mint authority

Permission to increase the supply of that mint. If the card says revoked and the field was read on-chain, the field that was read is empty (no authority set). If it says active, the permission is still assigned to an address.

Example. In the 2026-10-08 batch, the registry mint and the clones have it revoked. USDC has it active.

It does not let you conclude: Revoked does not identify the mint, does not show that the project is legitimate, and says nothing about demand or liquidity. An unavailable field is not filled in as revoked.

Freeze authority

Permission to freeze accounts of that token. “Revoked” means that specific permission was removed at the time of reading. Active means it is still assigned.

Example. In the same batch, the registry mint has it revoked and USDC has it active, assigned to an address the card shows.

It does not let you conclude: It is not a guarantee or proof of identity. The absence of this permission does not rule out other restrictions on the token.

Mutable metadata

If there is an update authority or is_mutable is true, the name, symbol, or image can change. “Not mutable in the sources read” describes those sources on that date.

Example. The registry mint, on 2026-10-08, is recorded as not mutable in the sources read. USDC has is_mutable true in Metaplex.

It does not let you conclude: That they cannot be changed in that reading does not show legitimacy. That they can be changed is not, by itself, impersonation.

Unknown and unavailable

Unavailable means that call did not leave a usable fact. Unknown is what was not read. Neither one is a value read on-chain.

Example. On all five cards from 2026-10-08, getTokenLargestAccounts returned HTTP 429. The token account sample stays unavailable. It is not zero concentration.

It does not let you conclude: It does not become zero, a revoked authority, or immutable metadata. A partial card also does not cancel the fields that were read on-chain.

Pump.fun curve

If the derived account belongs to the curve program, Verify reads its public fields. Classic progress is an inferred calculation, not a field of the account. If there is no curve, the curve amounts (Pump.fun calls these ‘reserves’) are not filled in with zero.

Example. On 2026-10-08 the registry mint has inferred progress 1.74. The clones have inferred 0.00 because the real curve amount matches the documented initial amount. USDC is not a curve: progress stays not applicable.

It does not let you conclude: Progress does not say what to do. Inferred 0.00 does not mean the account was missing. This mission does not simulate a swap.

Possible impersonation

A Verify signal: the name, symbol, image, or a link matches the curated registry and the mint is a different one. The match does not attribute intent.

Example. A token that copies the name or symbol of a well-known one, at a different address, carries that signal. USDC, read at its real address, does not.

It does not let you conclude: It is not a verdict or proof of what someone meant to do. A similar name, by itself, also does not replace reading the address.
